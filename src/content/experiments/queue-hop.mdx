## Who did what

Five of us built Queue Hop for ENPH 454, the Engineering Physics design course. Roles as the final report lists them:

- **Alex**: machine-learning architect, data analyst
- **Bryn**: electrical systems (the doorway capacity sensor)
- **Jonah**: machine-learning architect, cloud
- **Julia**: machine-learning architect, safety officer
- **Me**: software and app design. I built the React Native app, carried the model's output to it through the Google Sheets API, and helped with the P2PNet model.

## How it worked

A camera on a tripod looked down on the line through a window, and a laptop saved a snapshot every 10 seconds. The pipeline grabbed frames with PyAutoGUI, preprocessed them with PyTorch, ran P2PNet, and wrote the count to a Google Sheet that the app read: 10.2 seconds end to end.

P2PNet predicts a point per head rather than a density map, and it had to hold up whether the line was sparse or packed. We trained it on 998 annotated Clark Hall frames plus the ShanghaiTech crowd dataset, and held 500 frames back to test it. Night frames were dropped: window glare made them unusable.

The app has three screens: home, venue details and profile. Clark Hall Pub is the only venue wired to real data; the others are filler. Wait time is the head count multiplied by 72 seconds, the average time per person we measured over 221 observations.

## What didn't get done

The wait-time estimate was built but never validated against real waits: the one goal in the brief we missed. And while each subsystem (camera, model, door sensor, app) worked on its own, full end-to-end integration was left as future work.
